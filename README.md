# The Republic of Texas Debt Settlement, 1850-1856

A reconstructed dataset and analysis of how the Republic of Texas's revenue debt was scaled by the State of Texas and then settled by the U.S. Congress.

**Authors:** Nandan Ramaswamy 
**Contact:** contact.nandanr@gmail.com

## Research question
Texas scaled each debt instrument by its estimated value when issued (its "principle of equivalents" rating). Rated tranches range from 20 to 87.45 cents on the dollar, and two small tranches were rated at par. Congress instead appropriated one sum, $7.75 million, against a revenue debt of about $10.08 million, or about 76.9 cents on the dollar. Which instruments gained or lost relative to the state's own ratings?

## Data
- `data/debt_instruments.csv`: nine rows covering six instruments (PA_BANK, NAVAL, FUND1837, FUND1840, NOTES, TREAS8), with final principal plus interest, state ratings, and Miller page references.
- `data/purpose_of_debt_1836.csv`: what the Republic's early debt financed, as of 26 August 1836.
- `data/DATA_DICTIONARY.md`: column definitions.
- `data/SOURCES.md`: sources, access dates, and the proration evidence.

All figures are transcribed from secondary sources: E. T. Miller, *A Financial History of Texas* (1916), and Miller's *Handbook of Texas* entry. No archival records were used for numbers.

## Verification status
- `purpose_of_debt_1836.csv`: every row was independently re-entered and matched.
- `debt_instruments.csv`: every row was independently verified 
- The 1836 categories sum to $1,252,000, but Miller prints a total of $1,250,000. The CSV reproduces the printed components; do not use the stated total in calculations.

PA_BANK is the only instrument that receives less under the flat federal rate than under the state's own rating. The other five receive more. The largest dollar gains go to the treasury notes (NOTES, about $1.25 million) and the 1840 treasury bonds (TREAS8, about $0.81 million), followed closely by FUND1840 (about $0.79 million).

## Key assumption and limitations
- **Uniform proration.** The federal payment is modeled as a flat percentage of each instrument's final principal plus interest. Miller (1916, pp. 63, 64, 74, 79) describes pro rata payment at 76 9/10 cents on the dollar under the Act of February 28, 1855. - **Secondary-source numbers.** All amounts come from Miller and the TSHA entry.
- **Remainder resolved.** The former unitemized $1,417,680.00 is the 8% treasury bonds of February 5, 1840 (TREAS8), per Miller pp. 78-79. With this row, the instruments sum to the published revenue-debt total.
- **No present-value adjustment.** All amounts are nominal, as of final settlement.
- **Holders not covered.** The analysis compares instruments, not who ultimately held them.
